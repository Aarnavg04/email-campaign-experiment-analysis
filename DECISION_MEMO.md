# Which e-mail campaign should we send, and to whom?

**To:** Marketing lead **From:** Aarnav Gandhi **Date:** 15 August 2026
**Test:** 64,000 customers, three groups — Mens e-mail, Womens e-mail, no e-mail. Outcomes
measured over the two weeks after send.

---

## 1. Recommendation

**Send the Mens campaign to the entire list. The data does not support sending different
campaigns to different audiences.**

Stop sending the Womens campaign. It beats sending nothing, but the Mens campaign beat it
in every customer group we examined — including women's-merchandise buyers.

## 2. The numbers

Per **100,000 customers e-mailed**, compared with sending nothing:

| Campaign | Extra purchases | Extra revenue |
|---|---|---|
| **Mens** | **675** (plausibly 495–855) | **$66,000 – $77,000** |
| Womens | 314 (plausibly 152–475) | $38,000 – $42,000 |
| **Mens advantage over Womens** | **369 extra purchases** | — |

Purchase rate rose from 0.57% to 1.25% under the Mens campaign, and to 0.88% under Womens.

The revenue range reflects how much weight we give a handful of very large orders: the
lower figure caps the largest 0.1% of orders, the higher leaves them untouched. Both are
clearly positive, so the result does not rest on a few outliers.

Nearly all the gain comes from **more people buying**, not from buyers spending more —
average order value was unchanged at about $114.

## 3. Confidence, and why

**High confidence in the direction and rough size.**

- **The assignment genuinely worked.** The three groups came out the right size and matched
  each other on every characteristic we could measure beforehand — spend history, recency,
  channel, tenure.
- **The Mens result is about 2.6× larger than the smallest effect this test could reliably
  detect.** It is not a borderline call, and it survives capping the largest orders and
  analysing the data three different ways.
- **The analysis plan was published before anyone saw the results** — which measure counts,
  which customer groups to examine, what size of effect would justify acting. That is what
  separates this from the one slice out of many that happened to look good.

**Lower confidence in the Womens campaign's exact value:** its effect sits closer to the
edge of what this test can resolve.

## 4. What we cannot conclude

- **Nothing here measures list health** — no unsubscribes, complaints, or fatigue. A
  campaign that lifts sales this quarter while burning the list would look exactly like
  this one. **This is the biggest gap and should be closed before any permanent increase in
  send volume.**
- **We only see two weeks.** Some purchases may have been pulled forward rather than
  created.
- **Two groups were too small to judge** — customers who bought both men's and women's
  merchandise, and multichannel customers. Seeing nothing there means *we could not tell*,
  not *there is no effect*.
- **We tested one version of each e-mail.** "This Womens creative underperformed" is not
  "women's-targeted messaging doesn't work." That question is still open.
- The data is from a 2008 retail programme; response behaviour has moved on.

## 5. Recommended next step

**Do not run a test to confirm the Mens-vs-Womens gap among women's-merchandise buyers.**
That is where the campaigns came closest, so it is the tempting follow-up — but the gap is
small enough that resolving it reliably would need roughly **760,000 customers, about
twelve times this entire experiment.** Knowing that in advance saves the spend.

Instead, in priority order:

1. **Instrument the guardrails** — unsubscribe, complaint, and 90-day revenue tracking.
   This is the real unknown and it is cheap to fix.
2. **Ship the Mens campaign to the full list**, monitoring those guardrails.
3. **If women's-targeted messaging is worth pursuing, test a new creative** against the
   Mens campaign — a different execution, not the same one again.

Size any future test on conservative estimates, not on the best result we observed. The
strongest-looking group here was also the smallest, and such results systematically
flatter. Sizing a follow-up on that figure would leave it roughly **2.7× too small**.
